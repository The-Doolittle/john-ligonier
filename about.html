About the Author & Historian

Ralph Kinney Bennett (1941–2024) lived a life defined by a deep curiosity about the world and an enduring love for his home. Born at Latrobe Hospital and raised in Rector, Pennsylvania, Ralph's roots in the Ligonier Valley ran deep. His mother, Virginia, was the editor of the Ligonier Echo in the 1970s, establishing a family legacy of storytelling that Ralph would carry onto the national stage.

After graduating from Allegheny College in 1963 alongside his beloved wife, Ginny, Ralph embarked on a prestigious 40-year career in journalism. He reported for the Greensburg Tribune-Review, New Haven Register, Philadelphia Inquirer, and National Observer before joining the Washington bureau of The Reader’s Digest. For over thirty years as a senior writer and editor, he traveled across the U.S., Europe, and Central America, interviewing global figures ranging from Martin Luther King Jr. to Ronald Reagan.

Yet, no matter how far his assignments took him, he was always drawn back to Ligonier. Retiring in 2002, he returned home and immediately dedicated himself to the community's preservation. He joined the Ligonier Volunteer Hose Company #1 and served on the board of trustees for Fort Ligonier.

Combining his lifelong passion for military history with his exceptional talent as a writer, he authored "Ligonier 250: The Fort – The Town – The Valley" for the fort’s 250th anniversary in 2008. Often writing under the pseudonym John Ligonier, Ralph left behind an invaluable archive of historical research, ensuring the story of the valley he loved will never be forgotten.
